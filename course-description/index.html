    Title¶

    Spanish I

    Audience¶

    High school students in ISD 197 (Mendota Heights, MN) taking their first full year of Spanish. This course combines what was formerly taught as two separate semester courses, Spanish IA and Spanish IB, into a single year-long course delivered across four quarters. Students are true beginners: no prior formal Spanish instruction is assumed, though some students may arrive with informal home or community exposure to the language. The course follows the Entreculturas Book 1 curriculum (Unidades 1–4) and targets ACTFL Novice-level proficiency by the end of the year.

    Prerequisites¶

    • Age-appropriate (grades 9–12) literacy in English
    • No prior Spanish coursework required
    • Willingness to engage in guided oral communication from day one (the course begins producing simple spoken/written Spanish immediately rather than delaying output until grammar is "mastered")

    Topics¶

    1. Greetings and formal vs. informal address
    2. Personal identity and self-description (the verb ser)
    3. Expressing likes and dislikes with gustar + infinitives
    4. School vocabulary, schedules, and daily routines
    5. Expressing existence with indefinite articles and hay
    6. Definite articles and referring to people, places, and things
    7. Present-tense -ar verbs and everyday activities
    8. Family vocabulary and describing where family lives
    9. Descriptive adjectives and adjective agreement
    10. Location and the verb estar
    11. Present-tense -er and -ir verbs
    12. Food, meals, and market/shopping vocabulary
    13. Stem-changing verbs (e → ie) for requests and recommendations
    14. Expressing preferences with gustar and encantar
    15. Demonstrative adjectives
    16. Cultural comparison: identity and greetings in Paraguay and Texas
    17. Cultural comparison: school and learning in Costa Rica and California
    18. Cultural comparison: family and home life in Spain and Colorado
    19. Cultural comparison: food traditions in Mexico and North Carolina
    20. Intercultural communication: comparing Spanish-speaking regions to the students' own Minnesota community

    Topics Not Covered¶

    To keep this course's scope well-defined, the following are explicitly out of scope for Spanish I and are deferred to later courses in the ISD 197 sequence (Spanish II–IV):

    • Preterite and imperfect past tenses (introduced in Spanish II)
    • Future and conditional tenses (introduced in Spanish II–III)
    • Subjunctive mood in any form (introduced in Spanish III–IV)
    • Reflexive verbs and present progressive (introduced in Spanish II)
    • Formal commands, double object pronouns, por vs. para (introduced in Spanish II–III)
    • Extended literary readings or authentic long-form video series (e.g. El Gran Hotel, used starting in Spanish IV)
    • Heritage-speaker or AP-level rapid-immersion instruction

    Why This Course Matters¶

    Spanish I is the foundation of ISD 197's secondary world-language sequence. It is a student's first sustained experience producing another language, and the ACTFL-aligned, proficiency-based approach used here (communicate first, refine accuracy over time) sets expectations for every course that follows through Spanish IV. The paired cultural-comparison structure — each unit pairs a Spanish-speaking country or region with a U.S. state or the students' own Minnesota community — is deliberately built to develop the ACTFL Comparisons and Cultures standards alongside Communication, so students leave the course able to both communicate in Spanish and reason about how culture and language shape each other.

    Learning Outcomes¶

    After this course, students will be able to:

    • Remember:
    • Recall greeting and introduction vocabulary and recognize when formal versus informal address is required.
    • List the regular present-tense conjugation endings for -ar, -er, and -ir verbs.

    • Recognize core vocabulary sets for school schedules, family members, and everyday foods.

    • Understand:

    • Explain the difference between ser and estar and identify which situations call for each.
    • Explain how gustar-type verbs invert the usual subject-verb-object pattern used with regular Spanish verbs.

    • Describe how definite articles, indefinite articles, and hay differ in what they express about existence versus specific identity.

    • Apply:

    • Use present-tense -ar, -er, and -ir verbs to describe a typical school day and weekend activities.
    • Use adjective agreement and demonstrative adjectives to describe people, family members, and objects accurately.

    • Use stem-changing verbs and gustar/encantar to make requests, recommendations, and express food preferences in the market.

    • Analyze:

    • Compare greeting customs and self-introduction norms in Paraguay and Texas with local Minnesota practices.
    • Compare school schedules and approaches to learning in Costa Rica and California with the students' own school experience.

    • Compare family structures and food traditions across Spain, Mexico, and the students' own community to identify shared patterns and meaningful differences.

    • Evaluate:

    • Judge which register (formal or informal) and vocabulary set best fits a given communicative scenario, such as meeting a new teacher versus a classmate.
    • Assess whether a peer's spoken or written self-introduction correctly applies ser, adjective agreement, and appropriate register.

    • Critique a sample menu or shopping list for whether it accurately reflects stated food preferences using gustar and encantar.

    • Create:

    • Produce an original spoken self-introduction at the ACTFL Novice level, including identity, preferences, and appropriate register.
    • Design and present a digital family album that names and describes family members and where they live.
    • Create and present a personalized menu that expresses food preferences and traditions, incorporating market vocabulary and stem-changing verbs.
